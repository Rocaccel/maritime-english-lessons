# **Types of Ships**

All ships are divided into two broad categories: men-of-war and merchant ships.

Merchant ships can be classified according to what they carry. Most are designed to carry cargo, but a few still carry passengers.

Cargo ships can be divided into two basic types. One type carries dry cargo, the other carries liquid cargo; however, an OBO ship is designed to carry both.

In comparison with cargo vessels, passenger ships are fewer in number and type. The traditional passenger ship is the passenger liner; however many carry cargo as well. Nowadays their number has been greatly reduced, because of competition from air transport. Another type of passenger vessel is the cruise ship. These are similar in appearance to passenger liners. The most common type of passenger vessel is the ferry. Many of them are also designed to carry vehicles; therefore, these have doors at the stern or bow.

On one hand, all cargo ships can be divided into two types: dry cargo ships and tankers. On the other hand, cargo ships may be divided into universal ships designed to carry principal different types of cargo and specialized ships designed to carry one type of cargo e.g. bulk cargo, timber, refrigerated goods, oil, etc.

Universal vessels can carry practically any type of cargoes, including refrigerated goods and liquids (in special tanks). However, most of them are designed to carry certain types of goods. Thus, liners are intended to carry general cargo, packaged cargo, containers and special, liquid and refrigerated cargoes. Tramps are designed to carry bulk cargoes and timber. These ships must be of sufficient size to carry a profitable cargo and must be able to cope with bad weather conditions in any ocean; they must not be too large to enter the smallest ports of the world. The modern tramp travels at between 12 and 15 knots.

The use of container ships has continued to increase in recent years. Containerization means the consolidation of goods of single or mixed commodity into a freight container which may be 10 ft, 20 ft, 30 ft or 40 ft in length by 8 ft wide and 9 ft high in external cross section. The hull of a container ship is an enormous warehouse divided into cells by vertical guide rails. The cells are designed to hold containers. The containers carried on deck are carefully secured to ensure that they will not shift. The advantages of carrying cargo in containers are: short lay time because of efficient and rapid cargo handling; few stevedores are required; less pilferage because the cargo has been stored in locked containers. Each container can easily be found. Container ships are sometimes equipped with their own gantry cranes that load and discharge the cargo.

Roll-on/Roll-off vessels are designed to carry all sorts of rolled vehicles and motor cars by driving them on/off the ship. The advantages of Ro-Ro vessels:
1. independence of port facilities; 
2. quick door-to-door service; 
3. faster cargo handling rate; 
4. the risk of damage of pilferage is reduced.

Bulkers are single-decked ships, i.e. ships with no tweenecks in their holds but fitted with various types of special cargo handling equipment. The cargo is often loaded straight into the hold and discharged by grabs or other bulk handling methods. Bulkers carry cement, grain, ores, coal, sugar, bauxite, etc.

Tankers are especially designed to carry liquids (mainly oil products) in bulk. Tankers are equipped with pipes, valves and pumps to transfer cargo from/to the shore and from one tank to another when necessary. A tanker spends most of time at sea, as the loading and discharging of liquids is effected very fast. Today, large tankers can measure more than 1,500 feet long and 200 feet wide. Such tankers have various economic advantages over smaller tankers. It costs much less to ship a large amount of oil in one supertanker than in many small tankers. However, their huge size makes them difficult to navigate and increases the risk of accidents.

Two types of liquid bulk carrier of growing importance are Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) Carriers and chemical carriers.
